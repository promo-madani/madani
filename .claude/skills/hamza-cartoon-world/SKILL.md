## Core insight

"Dhongi Baba" won because of **story packaging**, not the baba himself:
curiosity + danger + family stakes + villain + consequence, readable in one
second from the thumbnail, with the outcome hinted in the title.

> Copy the storytelling engine, not the topic.

Positioning: **story-first Islamic family entertainment** — "Islamic values
hidden inside exciting modern stories." Every episode = Hamza in an
interesting problem/mystery/adventure/family situation, and the child learns
something by the end. The lesson is the purpose of the content, not the title.

## Content mix (next 2–3 months)

- 65% suspenseful family/moral stories (mystery, scam, family danger)
- 20% kids' real-life modern problems (school, money, phone, health)
- 10% travel / science / "Allah Pak Ki Nishaniyan" told as a story
- 5% direct promotional or standalone seasonal (Kalam/Naat)

Don't drop Kalam/Naat — weave them organically into emotional Hamza stories
instead of standalone uploads. Promotional/order videos stall around 1K; keep
the main channel's expectation story-first.

Build a **cluster** from Dhongi Baba so YouTube can chain recommendations:

New ideas should fit: **Hamza Mystery + Family Problem + Adventure +
Islamic/Moral Solution**. Mysteries must resolve logically (no real
supernatural scares for kids).

## Screenplay formula

- 0–10s: no logo, no long intro, no explanation. Open on the problem:
  "Hamza! Foran paise bhejo… main mushkil mein hoon!" → "Yeh to Dada Jan ki
  awaaz hai!" → cut → "Magar Dada Jan to mere saamne baithe hain!"
- First ~25%: the trap. Next 25%: complication. Third part:
  investigation/clues. Final part: reveal + a moral that comes out of the
  story's outcome — never a lecture tacked on at the end.
- Watch the first-30-second retention in Analytics.

## Title formula

**Character + Problem + Curiosity**, important words first, short, accurate.
Branding (`| Hamza Cartoon World`) only at the end, if at all.

- Good: "Dada Jan Ko Kya Ho Gaya?", "Hamza Ne OTP Bata Diya?", "Ghar Mein
  Chor Kaise Aaya?", "Is Box Ke Andar Kya Tha?"
- Avoid: "… | Hamza Cartoon World | Kids Video | Animated Cartoon Series"
  stuffing in front of the hook.
- Never false clickbait — the title's event must happen in the episode.
- Travel: attach the location to a question/mystery/challenge.
  "Hamza Norway Tour" → "Norway Mein Raat Hui Hi Nahi! Hamza Hairan Reh Gaya";
  "River Nile Tour" → "Darya-e-Neel Ka Pani Kahan Se Aata Hai? Hamza Ka Safar";
  "Hamza Visits Egypt" → "Hamza Pyramid Ke Andar Kya Dekhne Wala Hai?"

## Thumbnail rules

- Max 3 focal points: Hamza (big reaction face) + danger/key object +
  antagonist or reaction. One emotion, one danger, one question.
- **Thumbnail text must define the story on its own** — a complete phrase
  that names the subject (who / what / where), never a half phrase that only
  makes sense with the title. Usually 3–6 words, a question or exclamation.
- **Thumbnail text is always in Urdu script (اردو), not Roman Urdu or
  English.** Titles stay in Roman Urdu (search), thumbnail text in Urdu.
  Use common Urdu spellings; English loanwords written in Urdu (پارسل، ویڈیو،
  لفٹ). Keep brand-like acronyms (QR) as is.
  - ❌ "یہ کس نے بھیجا؟" → ✅ "پارسل کس نے بھیجا؟"
  - ❌ "اندر کیا ہے؟" → ✅ "اہرامِ مصر کے اندر کیا ہے؟"
  - ❌ "کھو گیا!" → ✅ "حمزہ ناروے میں کھو گیا!"
  - ❌ "کون ہے؟" → ✅ "لفٹ میں کون ہے؟"
  - ❌ "جعلی کال!" → ✅ "دادا جان کی جعلی کال!"
  - Test: someone who sees only the thumbnail (no title) must understand the
    story question. Still short enough to read on a phone — never the whole
    title, never over Hamza's face. Text and title should complement each
    other, not repeat word for word.
- Background tells the story, not decoration.
- Don't change a thumbnail/title that is already working (e.g. Dhongi
  Baba). Re-package only videos with impressions but low CTR.

### House thumbnail style (from the channel's own thumbnails, Oct 2026)

This is the look the user wants on every thumbnail — match it:

- **Characters:** glossy Pixar-style 3D, big round shiny eyes, rosy cheeks.
  - Hamza: white turban, white kurta/shalwar kameez, **red scarf around the neck** (signature item).
  - Dada Jan: long white beard, white turban, round glasses, white kurta, warm kind face.
  - Little sister (when present): white hijab, white dress.
  - Villains (thief, Dhongi Baba, scammer): exaggerated cartoon — sly grin, raised eyebrow, dark/earthy clothes, black mask for thieves.
- **Text:** Urdu script (right-to-left), huge bold 3D lettering in a thick, heavy Urdu display style (bold Naskh or chunky Nastaliq),
  fill = white or yellow→orange gradient, **thick red or dark-blue outline** plus a
  dark drop shadow; 2–3 stacked lines, slightly arched/tilted, bouncy baseline;
  occupies the top-right ~40–50% of the frame. Yellow burst/motion lines and
  sparkles around it, sometimes an emoji-style icon inside the text (thief mask,
  flag, question mark). Optional "WOW!" exclamation for adventure episodes.
- **Composition:** Hamza (and family) left / bottom-left, big and reacting;
  villain or key object right; text top-right. Hamza often points toward the
  subject or looks at it.
- **Background:** bright, saturated, detailed but readable — real location
  (Norway fjord village with flag, cozy home, park, shop), warm sunlight,
  soft depth of field. Not plain, not cluttered.
- **Mood:** high-energy, colorful, family-friendly; even "danger" episodes stay
  bright and warm, never dark or scary.

KEY SUBJECT on the RIGHT, clearly visible.
TEXT in the TOP-RIGHT area, taking about 40–50% of the frame, never covering any face.
Maximum 3 main elements: Hamza (+ family), key subject, text.

THUMBNAIL TEXT STYLE:

Huge, heavy, bold Urdu display lettering (thick bold Naskh / chunky Nastaliq style) rendered as glossy 3D text.
Fill: white or bright yellow-to-orange gradient. Thick red (or dark blue) outline, dark drop shadow, slight glossy highlight.

Yellow burst lines and small sparkles around the text; optionally one small emoji-style icon related to the story beside or inside the text.
Correct Urdu spelling and letter joining, crisp edges, readable on a small mobile screen.

BACKGROUND & LIGHTING:

Strong rim light separating characters from the background.

STYLE:
Glossy premium 3D Pixar-inspired animation, smooth skin shading, detailed fabric and textures, vibrant high-contrast colors, professional YouTube kids thumbnail, high energy, strong emotion, instantly understandable story.

IMPORTANT:
The viewer must understand the story from the image and text alone within one second.
Create curiosity without showing the solution.
No extra text besides TEXT. No misspelled letters. No duplicate characters.

Example texts (Urdu, complete, story-defining): دادا جان کی جعلی کال!،
پارسل کس نے بھیجا؟، اہرامِ مصر کے اندر کیا ہے؟، یہ QR کوڈ اسکین مت کرنا!،

حمزہ ناروے میں کھو گیا!

**Urdu text with AI image tools:** most image generators misspell or break
Urdu letters. Always also give the "no-text" fallback: replace the THUMBNAIL
TEXT STYLE block with "Leave the top-right area empty (clean sky/wall) for
text to be added later. Do not render any text or letters." Then the text is
added in Canva/Photoshop/Pixellab with a bold Urdu font (e.g. Noto Nastaliq
Urdu Bold, Jameel Noori Nastaleeq, or a heavy Naskh), yellow→orange fill,
thick red outline, drop shadow, slight arc — matching the house style.

When asked for a thumbnail, output the full master prompt with every [ ]
field filled in (always in the house style above, text in Urdu script),
the no-text fallback line, plus a one-line layout
note (left / right / text position).

## Shorts

From every major episode cut 3 Shorts: (1) cliffhanger ("Dada Jan ki awaaz
phone par bhi aur kamre mein bhi?"), (2) funny/shocking moment, (3) a
question to the viewer ("Agar koi phone par OTP maange to aap kya karenge?").
End each by pointing to the full episode.

## 30-day experiment & metrics

10 videos: 5 fraud/mystery/family-danger, 3 everyday kids' problems, 2
travel/adventure. For each, check:

- Home + Suggested CTR → packaging strength
- First-30-second retention → opening strength
- Average percentage viewed → story strength
- Returning viewers → Hamza brand strength
- Subscribers gained per video → which concepts build an audience, not just views

## Guardrails

- Kids' content: no real violence, gore or genuinely frightening imagery;
  scams and villains are shown, then defeated, with a safe takeaway.
- Keep Hamza's design consistent; avoid mass-produced, repetitive episodes
  (YouTube's inauthentic-content policy).
