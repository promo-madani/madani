## Core insight

"Dhongi Baba" won because of **story packaging**, not the baba himself:
curiosity + danger + family stakes + villain + consequence, readable in one
second from the thumbnail, with the outcome hinted in the title.

> Copy the storytelling engine, not the topic.

Positioning: **story-first Islamic family entertainment** — "Islamic values
hidden inside exciting modern stories." Every episode = Hamza in an
interesting problem/mystery/adventure/family situation, and the child learns
something by the end. The lesson is the purpose of the content, not the title.

## Content mix (next 2–3 months)

- 65% suspenseful family/moral stories (mystery, scam, family danger)
- 20% kids' real-life modern problems (school, money, phone, health)
- 10% travel / science / "Allah Pak Ki Nishaniyan" told as a story
- 5% direct promotional or standalone seasonal (Kalam/Naat)

Don't drop Kalam/Naat — weave them organically into emotional Hamza stories
instead of standalone uploads. Promotional/order videos stall around 1K; keep
the main channel's expectation story-first.

Build a **cluster** from Dhongi Baba so YouTube can chain recommendations:
Dhongi Baba → Fake Hakeem → Fake Call → Online Scam → Fake Charity → Fake
Video. Link them with a playlist, end screens (if not Made for Kids) and the

New ideas should fit: **Hamza Mystery + Family Problem + Adventure +
Islamic/Moral Solution**. Mysteries must resolve logically (no real
supernatural scares for kids).

## Screenplay formula

- 0–10s: no logo, no long intro, no explanation. Open on the problem:
  "Hamza! Foran paise bhejo… main mushkil mein hoon!" → "Yeh to Dada Jan ki
  awaaz hai!" → cut → "Magar Dada Jan to mere saamne baithe hain!"
- First ~25%: the trap. Next 25%: complication. Third part:
  investigation/clues. Final part: reveal + a moral that comes out of the
  story's outcome — never a lecture tacked on at the end.
- Watch the first-30-second retention in Analytics.

## Title formula

**Character + Problem + Curiosity**, important words first, short, accurate.
Branding (`| Hamza Cartoon World`) only at the end, if at all.

- Good: "Dada Jan Ko Kya Ho Gaya?", "Hamza Ne OTP Bata Diya?", "Ghar Mein
  Chor Kaise Aaya?", "Is Box Ke Andar Kya Tha?"
- Avoid: "… | Hamza Cartoon World | Kids Video | Animated Cartoon Series"
  stuffing in front of the hook.
- Never false clickbait — the title's event must happen in the episode.
- Travel: attach the location to a question/mystery/challenge.
  "Hamza Norway Tour" → "Norway Mein Raat Hui Hi Nahi! Hamza Hairan Reh Gaya";
  "River Nile Tour" → "Darya-e-Neel Ka Pani Kahan Se Aata Hai? Hamza Ka Safar";
  "Hamza Visits Egypt" → "Hamza Pyramid Ke Andar Kya Dekhne Wala Hai?"

## Thumbnail rules

- Max 3 focal points: Hamza (big reaction face) + danger/key object +
  antagonist or reaction. One emotion, one danger, one question.
- Text 2–4 words only, never the full title, never over Hamza's face.
- Background tells the story, not decoration.
- Don't change a thumbnail/title that is already working (e.g. Dhongi
  Baba). Re-package only videos with impressions but low CTR.

### Thumbnail master prompt (fill in the [STORY] parts)

```
Create a highly clickable YouTube thumbnail for "Hamza Cartoon World" in premium 3D animated cartoon style, cinematic and family-friendly.

STORY: [one-line story situation]
EMOTION: [shocked / worried / curious / surprised / confused]
PROBLEM ELEMENT: [villain / suspicious object / phone / parcel / thief / scammer / warning symbol / mystery]
TEXT: "[2–4 WORDS]"

MAIN CHARACTER:
Hamza, a 10-year-old Muslim boy, wearing a clean white shalwar kameez, white turban fully covering his hair, black shoes, expressive innocent face. Keep Hamza's face and character design consistent with the original reference.

COMPOSITION:
Use only 2–3 main visual elements.
Place Hamza large in the foreground, occupying approximately 35–45% of the thumbnail.
Show a strong facial reaction matching EMOTION.
Place the PROBLEM ELEMENT clearly on the opposite side.
Create a strong visual conflict between Hamza and the problem.

CAMERA:
Medium close-up or close-up. Slight cinematic perspective. Eye-level camera. No extreme low angle.
Keep faces large and clearly readable even on a mobile screen.

LIGHTING:
Bright cinematic lighting. Strong subject separation. Soft rim light around Hamza.
Slightly darker or dramatic background behind the danger or mystery element.
High contrast but child-friendly. Clean, polished, premium 3D render.

BACKGROUND:
Simple and uncluttered. Only the environment necessary to explain the story.
Slight depth of field. No unnecessary people, objects, decorations, or busy details.

THUMBNAIL TEXT:
Only the TEXT above, 2–4 very large words. Bold, thick, highly readable 3D text.
Place it in an empty area and never cover Hamza's face. Readable on a small mobile screen.

VISUAL STYLE:
Premium 3D Pixar-inspired family animation quality, expressive characters, detailed textures, professional cinematic lighting, vibrant but controlled colors, polished YouTube kids thumbnail, strong storytelling, emotional clarity, high curiosity, instantly understandable visual story.

IMPORTANT:
The viewer should understand the central problem within one second.
Create curiosity without showing the complete solution.
One main emotion. One main danger or mystery. One clear story question.
No unnecessary text. No crowded composition. No duplicate characters.
No extra limbs or fingers. No distorted faces. No blurry image.
No watermark. No logo covering the scene.
16:9 YouTube thumbnail, 1280x720, ultra sharp, high detail, professional commercial quality.
```

Example short texts: FAKE CALL!, KAUN HAI?, YEH KYA HAI?, CHOR!, SCAM!,
MAT KARO!, DHONGI BABA!, SCAN MAT KARO!, YEH KIS NE BHEJA?

When asked for a thumbnail, output the master prompt with STORY, EMOTION,
PROBLEM ELEMENT and TEXT filled in, plus a one-line layout note
(left / center / right).

## Shorts

From every major episode cut 3 Shorts: (1) cliffhanger ("Dada Jan ki awaaz
phone par bhi aur kamre mein bhi?"), (2) funny/shocking moment, (3) a
question to the viewer ("Agar koi phone par OTP maange to aap kya karenge?").
End each by pointing to the full episode.

## 30-day experiment & metrics

10 videos: 5 fraud/mystery/family-danger, 3 everyday kids' problems, 2
travel/adventure. For each, check:

- Home + Suggested CTR → packaging strength
- First-30-second retention → opening strength
- Average percentage viewed → story strength
- Returning viewers → Hamza brand strength
- Subscribers gained per video → which concepts build an audience, not just views

## Guardrails

- Kids' content: no real violence, gore or genuinely frightening imagery;
  scams and villains are shown, then defeated, with a safe takeaway.
- Keep Hamza's design consistent; avoid mass-produced, repetitive episodes
  (YouTube's inauthentic-content policy).
